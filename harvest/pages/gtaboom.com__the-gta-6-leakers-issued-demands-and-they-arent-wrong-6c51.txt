GTA 6 Leaker Cyberleek Issues Three Demands To Take Two

By Ray Ampoloquio · Aug 18, 2026
Updated by Matt Gibbs · Aug 19, 2026

Cyberleek stole GTA 6 footage and is funding its "commandments" with a memecoin. Don't expect Take Two to negotiate, apologize, or pay restitution.

Zoom image: The Cyberleek group behind the GTA 6 leak issued three demands to Rockstar and Take-Two.
Cyberleek , the account that dumped stolen GTA 6 gameplay and a map image on August 18 while quietly running a memecoin on the side, has followed it up with three "commandments" for Rockstar Games , Take-Two Interactive , and every other big publisher, saying it is the bare minimum it will accept before it stops going after them.

The crazy part here is that if you look beyond the ones responsible for saying it, most Grand Theft Auto fans would agree with what they have to say and what they are fighting for.

Cyberleek argues nobody should pay for a game through a storefront before it is out and before reviews exist, and that if publishers want your money early, they can go back to pressing discs like the old days.

They're also arguing against fake single-player DLC , meaning publishers should stop charging you for content already sitting inside the game files you bought.

Finally, they're pushing for preservation. That single-player games should ship with an offline fallback so they still work if the servers get switched off, which is the same fight preservationists have been having over dead games like The Crew .

The grievances are real. GTA 6 is launching with no physical disc at all , publishers really do lock content behind paywalls that some players suspect was on the disc the whole time , games really do get killed off with the money kept and the servers pulled, the same concern that has Grand Theft Auto Online players nervous about their own community servers .

Cyberleek's three commandments, translated
The demand

What it actually means

The reality

No digital preorders

Stop letting people pay before launch and before reviews exist; press physical discs instead

Popular idea, zero chance a publisher adopts it under threat

No fake single-player DLC

Stop charging for content already sitting in the game files you bought

A real grievance, though "fake" is doing a lot of undefined work

Preserve single-player games

Ship a final offline patch so games still run if the servers die

The most defensible one, and closest to a real movement

Three demands most players would happily sign, attached to a hack and a memecoin nobody asked for.

The message, indeed, is popular. The problem? It's the messenger. What they're doing is still utterly wrong.

This is a group that stole footage, is threatening to keep hacking anyone who breaks its rules, insists a public apology is not enough because "restitution is mandatory," and is bankrolling the whole crusade with a cryptocurrency called $CYBERLEEK. The people demanding Take-Two stop treating fans like a revenue stream are, at the very same time, trying to use those same fans for a quick cash grab.

Will it work? Probably not. Take-Two is a public company. It doesn't negotiate with hackers and people responsible for data breaching because doing so opens a whole can of worms, inviting others to try and do the same. Don't expect an apology, a concrete commitment, or restitution. What will happen here are lawyers working overtime, takedown notices flying everywhere, and silence, which is already exactly what is happening. Extortion is a terrible delivery system for a wishlist, however agreeable the wishlist is.

By bolting genuinely reasonable demands onto theft, threats, and a crypto grift, Cyberleek is not advancing any of this. It is poisoning it. Ending pay-before-you-see-it preorders, real preservation and honest DLC are serious conversations that the industry needs to have. Handing them to a hacker with a memecoin lets publishers get away clean by giving them a villain to point to and changing the subject.

None of this changes anything. There's a reason why the GTA 6 boycott is a non-event . The people with real leverage here are not hackers issuing commandments, but the millions who have already paid full price. Until that changes, nothing will. Rockstar's Extended Look still premieres on Netflix on August 27 , GTA 6 still launches on November 19 , and these three commandments will become a footnote by the time either arrives.

The demands deserve a better champion than the one currently holding them hostage.
