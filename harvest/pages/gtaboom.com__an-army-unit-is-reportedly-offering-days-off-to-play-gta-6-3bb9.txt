An Army Unit Is Reportedly Offering Days Off to Play GTA 6

By Ray Ampoloquio · Aug 18, 2026
Updated by Matt Gibbs · Aug 18, 2026

A viral memo claims a Fort Stewart Army battalion offers reenlisting soldiers four days off starting the day after Grand Theft Auto 6 launches.

A memo making the rounds online claims that a U.S. Army battalion is offering its soldiers four days off to play Grand Theft Auto 6 as a reward for re-enlisting. The document, dated July 28, is addressed to everyone in the 9th Brigade Engineer Battalion, a real unit stationed at Fort Stewart, Georgia, laying out a "Special Reenlistment Incentive" tied directly to the next Grand Theft Auto .

It states that any active-duty enlisted soldier in the battalion who signs a new contract between August 1 and November 14 gets a special four-day pass running November 20 through 23, so the leave kicks in the very next morning GTA 6 comes out .

According to the letter, the goal is to boost retention and morale, which is a a bureaucratic way of saying the Army would like people to re-up and it noticed that a lot of them are huge fans of Rockstar Games ' best-selling franchise.

The catch? It isn't exactly a vacation. It is a short stretch of authorised time off, you need your company commander to sign off, you cannot bolt it onto other leave to stretch it into a real trip, and you are out entirely if you have administrative flags or you are failing physical fitness or body composition standards, which you might if you end up letting yourself go while going Kai Cenat on GTA 6 .

What checks out, and what doesn't
Checks out

Still unconfirmed

The 9th Brigade Engineer Battalion at Fort Stewart is a real unit

Lt. Col. Ryan Hodgson really is its commander, in the job since July 2026

Reporters reached out and could not authenticate the memo

The formatting and header details read as legitimate to enlisted soldiers

It could still be a hoax, just a fairly sophisticated one

Unit-level morale and retention perks are a normal, real thing

If genuine, it is one battalion's perk, not an Army-wide policy

The people, the unit, and the dates are all real. The memo itself isn't officially verified.

The image went viral because of the military meme page, "USA Army WTF!? Moments," and spread everywhere from there . Many outlets have reached out and couldn't confirm its authenticity. However, a suspicious number of details hold up, from the commander's name to the communication numbers in the header to formatting that enlisted soldiers say matches the real thing.

Lieutenant Colonel Ryan D. Hodgson, whose signature sits at the bottom, really did take command of the battalion back in July, and plenty of veterans online basically shrugged it off, since unit commanders have a reputation for offering creative time-off bribes to beef up re-enlistment numbers. Which brings us to the part that actually matters, real or not, and that's the fact that this is instantly believable.

Nobody blinked at the idea of the United States Army building a retention incentive around a video game launch because GTA is that game. We have already watched a 78-year-old grandmother buy an Xbox just to be there for it , companies float launch-day time off or fielding a skeletal workforce , and the entire video game industry rearranging their entire holiday season around it .

Even the official White House social media account used GTA in a recent video . When viewed from that perspective, a game reaching the point where the military might use it to keep soldiers in uniform is just the next logical, faintly absurd rung on that ladder.

Perhaps just for the lulz, the same meme page that posted the memo tagged Rockstar and openly asked the studio to send free games to any troops who re-up.

Whether the 9th BEB ever confirms this or it quietly turns out to be someone's very committed joke, the calendar doesn't care. The Extended Look still hits Netflix on August 27 , the game still lands November 19 , and somewhere in Georgia, a few soldiers are reportedly weighing four days in Leonida against another few years in the Army.
